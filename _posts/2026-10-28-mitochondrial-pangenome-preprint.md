---
title: "Preprint: a pangenomic view of plant mitochondrial genome evolution"
summary: Over 300 land plant mitochondrial genomes show conserved gene content but collapsing synteny, with genome size and repeats driving structural complexity.
---

Our preprint [*Conserved genes, discordant structure: a pangenomic view of plant mitochondrial genome evolution*](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.761860) is now out on bioRxiv.

Land plant mitochondrial genomes vary more than two orders of magnitude in size, from about 66 kb to over 19 Mb, and range from simple circles to highly complex, multipartite assemblies. We de novo assembled and annotated over 300 of them, measured their assembly graph complexity and recombination potential, and built a "hyper" pangenome spanning the land plant phylogeny.

Some highlights:

- **Gene content is highly conserved**, and so is gene order in mosses and liverworts. In vascular plants, synteny collapses almost entirely.
- **Genome size and interspersed repeats** are the strongest predictors of structural complexity.
- **DNA transfer into the mitochondrion** is roughly three times more frequent from the nuclear genome than from the plastid, but it does not itself drive genome expansion.
- **TE's** - we found lots of evidence of TE's in our mitochondrial genomes, but they were all non-functional even though in some lineages they had been copied in the mitochondrion post-transfer from the nucleus.
